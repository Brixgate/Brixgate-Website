01Why this field

Most wasted money is spent building the wrong thing correctly.

02What the work looks like

The whole job is on this page.

Somebody asks for something. Your job is to find out what they actually need and write it so a delivery team can build it without guessing. That translation is what twelve weeks buys you.

What you are handed

"We need a dashboard so management can see what is going on."

Reasonable sounding, and impossible to build. Which management, seeing what, deciding what, and how would anyone know if it worked?

No user named No decision No measure of done Solution, not problem
Twelve weeks
What you hand over

Regional managers need to spot stock running out before it does.

  • Who
    Eleven regional managers, checking on a phone, most often on a Monday morning.
  • Decision
    Whether to move stock between branches this week.
  • Needs
    Days of cover per item per branch, flagged under a threshold they set.
  • Done when
    A manager can answer "which branch do I move stock to" in under a minute.
  • Not doing
    Forecasting, supplier ordering, anything historical beyond twelve weeks.
03Who it is for

If you are the person who asks the awkward question, you already have the instinct.

This is you

Twelve weeks will suit you

  • You sit between the people who want things and the people who build them.
  • You like asking why, and are comfortable doing it more than once in a meeting.
  • You write clearly, or want to, because most of this job ends up on a page.
  • You can find five hours a week for three months.
This is not you

Look elsewhere in Foundations

  • You want to run the delivery rather than define it. Project Management is the neighbouring twelve weeks.
  • You want to answer questions with data yourself. Data Analytics runs eight weeks.
  • You would rather avoid meetings. A great deal of this job is conversations with people who disagree.
04The twelve weeks

One real problem, carried from first conversation to handover.

You pick a problem in week one and it stays with you. By week twelve you have the pack a delivery team could actually build from, and you have defended it to people who pushed back.

WEEKS 01 — 04

Finding out what is true

People describe solutions, not problems, and they describe the process they wish they followed rather than the one they do. Getting underneath that is a learnable technique.

InterviewingAsking so people tell you what actually happens.
Watching the workObserving the real process, not the documented one.
StakeholdersWho decides, who is affected, who is quietly ignored.
The real problemSeparating the ask from the need behind it.
WEEKS 05 — 08

Writing it down properly

A requirement that can be read two ways will be built the wrong way. This phase is about precision, and about drawing the process so disagreements surface on paper rather than in delivery.

RequirementsWritten so they cannot be read two ways.
Process mapsDrawing how work flows, and where it stalls.
AcceptanceDefining done before anybody starts building.
ScopeWriting down what you are deliberately not doing.
WEEKS 09 — 12

Making it survive contact

Where AI arrives, and where the work gets political. A pack is only good if it holds up when someone senior wants something else.
