02What the work looks like

Three decisions. The third one is the lesson.

Every product job is a run of these. Someone asks for something, you work out what is actually true, and you decide. The skill is not having good ideas. It is being wrong cheaply and knowing it early.

Built it

"Let people save a delivery address."

  • Asked by
    Support, who were retyping addresses on the phone all day.
  • Evidence
    Six of eight customers watched re-entered the same address every order.
  • Bet
    Repeat orders rise if checkout stops being a chore.
+14%repeat orders in eight weeks
Cut it down

"We need a full loyalty programme."

  • Asked by
    The CEO, after a competitor launched one.
  • Evidence
    Nobody interviewed mentioned points. Four mentioned delivery cost.
  • Bet
    Test free delivery over a threshold first. Two weeks, not five months.
2 wksinstead of five months, same question answered
Got it wrong

"Add product reviews."

  • Asked by
    Everyone. It was on every roadmap for a year.
  • Evidence
    Thin. It was obviously a good idea, so nobody checked.
  • Bet
    Reviews build trust and lift conversion.
0.4%of buyers left one. Shipped, and quietly wrong

Three decisions, and the third is the one worth studying. Nobody was careless; reviews really were a reasonable idea. The difference is that the first two were cheap to be wrong about and the third was not, because no one asked what evidence would have changed their mind. Weeks five to eight are about making that question a habit.

04The twelve weeks

One product decision, taken properly, from question to result.

You take a real product question in the first phase and test it against evidence in the second. We introduce the inconvenient findings deliberately, because a decision that is never challenged teaches nobody anything.

01
Weeks 1 — 4

Finding out what is true

People tell you what they think you want to hear, and usage data tells you what they did without telling you why. You learn to use both, and to notice when they disagree.

  • Interviewing users without leading them to your answer
  • Reading usage data, and what it cannot tell you
  • Separating what people ask for from what they need
  • Writing the question down before hunting an answer
02
Weeks 5 — 8

Choosing, and saying no

The core of the course. You have more requests than weeks, and most of them came from someone senior. Deciding is the job; defending the decision is the part nobody teaches.

  • Prioritising against evidence rather than volume
  • Telling someone senior their idea is not next
  • Cutting an idea down to the cheapest honest test
  • Writing down what would change your mind
03
Weeks 9 — 12

Measuring, and AI

Synthesising interview notes and sifting usage data is exactly the work worth handing to AI, so we do. The judgement about what it means stays yours.

  • Choosing the one number that answers the question
  • AI for synthesising interviews and sifting usage data
  • Where a good-looking number is misleading you
  • Saying plainly that a bet did not work
05What you leave with

A decision you can defend, and the evidence you used to reach it.
